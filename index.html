Domain of Shared Breath
Primary Stat: Constitution  ·  Custom Domain & Aspect
“Strength that lives between people, not within them.”
❧ ✦ ☙
Who this cleric is

Before the domain, there was just someone who noticed. Who tracked the person at the edge of the group — the one not quite laughing along, the one who drifted back when the danger passed. The instinct was not heroism. It was maintenance: an ongoing, low-level monitoring of the room, making sure no one was being overlooked.

The Breath Link is the mechanisation of that instinct. The two-link cap is the mechanisation of its cost — this cleric can hold most of the party, but not all of it. Someone is always left out. That is the mechanic because it was already true.

Overview

The domain's central mechanic is the Breath Link: a communal network of ambient awareness and small gifts, freely given, persistent until severed. Up to two may be held at once — at a standard party size, someone is always left out, and that scarcity is the mechanic.

The Anchor. Whoever does the work of forging and curating the network is called the Anchor. At Level 3, the cleric is the Anchor. From Level 4, when the Aspect's Bond forms, the role transfers: the cleric's wide reach narrows into devotion to one person, and the community-building hand passes to the Bond's Anchor. If no Bond is active, the cleric is again the Anchor and may forge links normally.

The Breathing Network
Level 3  ·  Domain Ability
Forge Breath Link Active · Bonus Action

As a Bonus Action, the Anchor touches a willing creature within reach to forge a Breath Link. The Anchor may maintain up to 2 Breath Links at once; forging a new one at cap drops an existing link of the Anchor's choice (free, no sensation). A link persists indefinitely until severed by the Anchor (free action, on their turn), the linked creature dies, or the cleric falls unconscious or dies. Once forged, a Breath Link's effects function regardless of distance, so long as the link persists.

Felt Presence Passive · Network

All network members sense one another through the link — a vague awareness of direction and proximity. Not exact location, not identity beyond who is already known to be linked. A felt sense of that way. When the network shatters, this awareness simply fades — an absence where there had been company. The link is invisible to outsiders; Detect Magic registers something vague between linked creatures but reveals nothing of structure or mechanics.

Steadied by the Link Passive · Breath Anchor Benefit

While Breath-linked, a creature has advantage on saving throws to resist or end the Frightened condition. They feel steadied — watched over — without knowing why. The Anchor does not receive this benefit. This is the enticement: the Anchor extends something genuinely good to whoever they choose to link.

Felt Across the Distance Passive

The cleric senses through the network at all times. Whenever any Breath-linked creature takes damage, the cleric feels it — not numbers, just impact. Direction, severity, distress. Distance is no barrier: if a Breath-linked creature is hurt, the cleric knows the moment it happens. This sense is the cleric's alone and does not transfer with the Anchor role.

Interpose Active · Reaction · CD Point

When a Breath-linked creature takes damage, the cleric may spend a Channel Divinity point as a Reaction to halve the damage. Declare after seeing the total, but before the target's resistances or immunities are applied. The halved damage retains its original type. Interpose can only target a Breath-linked creature.

The network terminating on the cleric's unconsciousness — not the Anchor's — is intentional. The Breath Link is an extension of the cleric's awareness: the mechanisation of that low-level monitoring instinct described in the backstory. The Anchor role (who can forge new links) can transfer, but the network itself is always the cleric's creation, sustained by the cleric's presence. The cleric is the root. This has a practical table consequence: the cleric going down is always catastrophic for the network, even when they're not the active Anchor. A frontliner Bond's Anchor can drop without the network shattering — a useful asymmetry that rewards keeping the cleric upright.
The Network Deepens
Level 6  ·  Domain Ability
Extended Reach Passive · Range

The Anchor may establish a Breath Link as a Bonus Action with a willing creature up to 30 feet away, provided the current Anchor has previously touched that creature to form a Breath Link (or, in the cleric's case, a Bond). For an entirely new creature, physical contact is still required to form the first link.

Wordless Impressions Passive · Network

The network's awareness deepens. Emotional texture now travels alongside the directional sense from Level 3 — only what the body cannot conceal. A mood. A weight. A separated party member's fear reaches the others before any signal could. Words, images, thought, and intent do not pass through; the network carries presence, not message.

Conduit Passive · Network

Once per turn, as a free object interaction, a network member may pass one held object to another network member within 60 feet. The object passes through the network — no line of sight or line of effect required. No action required from the recipient. A potion crosses the battlefield. A weapon reaches the disarmed ally. The network moves things the way breath moves — without ceremony.

The Last Breath
Level 17  ·  Domain Ability
Catch Active · Reaction · CD Point

When a Breath-linked creature would be reduced to 0 HP — including by effects that would kill outright — the cleric may spend a Channel Divinity point as a Reaction. The creature enters a temporary Stasis instead: Paralyzed, immune to all damage, untargetable. No death saving throws.

Stasis lasts 2 rounds. At the end of the second round, the creature exits automatically at 1 HP, all prior conditions removed. This ability may be used once per creature per long rest.

While in Stasis. The creature is suspended — aware, but unable to act or speak. Something reaches them from outside: steady, wordless, not urgent.

this is the same Stasis described in the Aspect section. The experience notes there apply generally, except that the Stased creature cannot push impressions outward (that requires an active Bond). Everything else — including what the outside character feels through the connection — applies as written.

About the Aspect

A cleric naturally has breadth — a wide spell list, abilities across roles. The Domain is already a specialization, but a clean one: it adds without taking away. The Aspect goes further. It narrows your spell access to what the domain truly represents, leans you fully into its spirit, and attaches the Flaw — rule-enforced compulsions that come with that commitment. The Domain alone is already complete; the Aspect is how far this character is willing to go.

Who this person already was

Someone who learned early that closeness had to be earned through utility — give before asked, absorb before offered. The Breath Link was already the shape of that reaching; the magic just gave it a name.

The Aspect arrived and narrowed it. The compulsion to protect everyone became the compulsion to protect this one, formalized into rules: a damage type, a Saving Throw, a range in feet. It offered exactly what they had always wanted — permanence, inescapability — and folded the disaster inside the gift. They will not understand the full cost until they find that being unreachable in Stasis is the most rest they have ever had.

The spell restriction

The cleric's magic should protect, sustain, or connect — not harm or diminish. The test: could this spell flow through a Breath Link? Bless can. Hold Person cannot.

Abjuration and enchantment on the cleric and paladin lists are a natural starting point — that's where most of it lives. But anything fitting the intent qualifies; the schools are a direction, not a fence. A campaign may relax this restriction, but this is the version that holds together.

What the Aspect costs

The Flaw — Mechanics make the character fail saves, shatter bonds, freeze when their Anchor walks away. No opting out at the table.

The Spell Restriction — The cleric draws from a narrow field: protection, sustain, connection. Damage and coercion were never learned.

The Psychology — You are playing a specific type of person now. The rules don't enforce the characterization, but it is part of the package.

The Anchor's Exchange
Level 4  ·  Aspect Ability
People-pleasing as self-harm. You give your life force freely, and call it love.

As an Action, make physical contact with a willing creature within reach. That creature becomes your Anchor. You may only have one Anchor at a time.

Bond ConditionsPassive · State

The Bond persists as long as you remain conscious, the Anchor is alive, and neither of you is more than 60 feet from the other. If any condition breaks, the Bond shatters silently. You may dissolve it voluntarily as a free action on your turn.

Life TransferActive · Reaction

When your Anchor takes damage, you may use your Reaction to intercept it — you take the damage instead. Declare after seeing the total, but before the Anchor's resistances or immunities are applied. The damage becomes Psychic regardless of its original type. The Anchor takes none.

This is visible. The cleric flinches, pales, absorbs the impact while the Anchor feels nothing. Witnesses who notice this pattern consistently may eventually piece it together — triggering discovery consequences under The Secret.

The Breaking PointPassive · Trigger

If intercepted damage exceeds half your maximum HP, the Bond shatters immediately afterward — after the damage is dealt, not before. The damage is never reduced by the break. You are immediately Frightened (Anchor is the source) until the end of your next turn.

This fear is not rational. That is the point.

At Level 8, the Permanent Bond cannot be shattered — but the Frightened condition still triggers. The tether holds. The cleric is afraid of the person they cannot leave.

Breath Links Domain Interaction

While the Bond is active, the Anchor takes over the Breath Link network. The cleric can no longer forge Breath Links. Only the Anchor can.

If the Bond breaks, all Breath Links shatter.

Psychic was chosen for two reasons. Mechanically: it bypasses most resistance systems, so the cleric pays full price regardless of the Anchor's build. Lorewise: the cleric takes psychic damage because that is literally what is happening — they feel their allies being hurt. The damage type is not an abstraction.
Shared Vitality Pool
Level 6  ·  Aspect Ability
You learn that love does not have to mean destruction. But you haven't learned to stop yet.

Your understanding of the Bond deepens. You no longer need proximity to initiate it — and you learn to weave a third presence into the connection: a living reserve of shared vitality drawn from both of you at once.

Extended RangePassive · Range

You may establish the Bond as an Action with a creature up to 30 feet away — but only with someone you have previously bonded with through direct touch. Level 4 persistence conditions still apply. To bond with someone entirely new, physical touch is still required.

The Vitality PoolPassive · Resource

The cleric carries a Shared Vitality Pool — a third resource, separate from either character's hit points. Maximum = (your CON modifier + Anchor's CON modifier) × proficiency bonus; recalculates whenever a new Anchor is bonded. Starts empty when first gained and persists across Anchor changes, rests, and combat. Both characters sense its current value instinctively — no explanation required.

Life Transfer (Upgraded)Active · Reaction

When Life Transfer triggers, damage is drawn from the Pool first. Only the excess beyond the Pool's remaining value is taken from your HP as Psychic damage. If the Pool absorbs everything, you feel nothing. If the Pool was empty, the full damage hits you as before.

DonateActive · Bonus Action

Either bonded character may donate up to their Constitution modifier in HP into the Pool. The Pool cannot exceed its maximum. The Anchor may do this on their own turn.

WithdrawActive · Bonus Action

Either bonded character may withdraw up to their Constitution modifier in HP from the Pool, healing themselves for that amount. Cannot exceed what the Pool holds. Donating and withdrawing cannot both occur on the same turn.

The Pool is a shared reserve — both characters choose whether to spend it offensively (buffering Life Transfer) or defensively (spot healing in a pinch).

Pool CollapsePassive · Trigger

If the Bond breaks while the Pool holds any HP, both the cleric and the former Anchor immediately take Psychic damage equal to the Pool's current HP (no saving throw). The Pool empties. A Pool at 2 HP barely stings. A Pool at 15 is a genuine detonation.

Collapse triggers on: voluntary dissolution with no new Bond established in the same Action; and Flaw-triggered breaks — the Breaking Point shattering the Bond, or the Ache of Absence dissolving it at dawn.

Collapse does not trigger if the cleric dissolves the Bond and establishes a new one in the same Action — the Pool carries across intact. Breath Links shatter regardless; the new Anchor rebuilds the network from scratch. Collapse also does not trigger on involuntary breaks from outside forces (enemy dispels, forced movement, environmental effects). It is reserved for breaks that come from within.

A cleric who needs to switch Anchors mid-combat can do so freely — but one who simply lets go, with no one to reach toward, pays the price of that emptiness.

Held AloftPassive

While the Pool holds at least 1 HP, both bonded characters have advantage on death saving throws.

The connection is a lifeline in more ways than one.

The pool is sized to feel significant — enough that losing it hurts, not so large it trivialises Life Transfer. Collapse was deliberately tied to relationship events rather than external ones; an enemy dispelling the pool would frame the cleric as a victim of circumstance. A cleric-triggered collapse — from voluntary dissolution or a flaw break — makes it the cost of their own codependency. The intent is for the pool to create tension in the relationship: the Anchor absorbing an aftershock they thought was being handled for them. Good intentions, at a cost to someone else.
The Eternal Tether
Level 8  ·  Aspect Ability
You wanted someone who would never leave. You found a way to make it impossible.

The Bond transcends choice. It settles into the fabric of you both — permanent, unbreakable by distance or circumstance.

Permanent BondPassive · State

Leveling up unlocks the ability to perform The Asking — a ritual that, if completed, makes the current Bond permanent. Until The Asking succeeds, full Level 6 mechanics remain in effect and Level 8 abilities (Resonance, Shared Fortitude, Stasis) remain locked.

Once permanent: the Bond persists regardless of distance, unconsciousness, or temporary death states. It cannot be broken by outside forces. This Bond cannot be dissolved. The flexibility of Levels 4 and 6 ends here. There is only this person, and whatever comes next.

The Asking
Ritual·10 minutes·Completes the Permanent Bond

The cleric must have an active Bond before beginning. The ritual transforms an existing Bond — it cannot create a new one.

The cleric initiates by making a small wound on their palm and extending their open hand toward the Anchor without a word. If the Anchor takes the hand and mirrors the wound, the ritual begins. Neither character needs to understand what is happening. One of them does.

The cleric then performs a gesture of their choosing — tracing a symbol on the Anchor's wrist, pressing joined hands to their chest, holding the silence. What matters is that it feels like a threshold. Something offered that cannot be taken back. There are no words, because the cleric cannot speak the truth of what this is.

Then the cleric goes still. And waits.

The ritual completes when the Anchor, of their own will, asks the cleric something personal — directed at them, about them, for them. Not logistics. Not tactics. Something that treats the cleric as a person whose inner life matters. If the Anchor doesn't ask — pulls away, fills the silence, or simply doesn't — the ritual quietly fails. No penalty. The cleric's hand closes around nothing, and the night continues.

If the Anchor has previously discovered the Flaw and requires verbal consent to form a Bond, the personal question during The Asking serves as that consent.

Example
[ evening. a fire. a long quiet. ]
— the cleric — "There is something I want to give you. I need you to let me."
[ the cleric draws the blade. extends their hand. the anchor takes it, mirrors the wound. joined hands, a gesture, and then the cleric goes still. ]
— the anchor — "I don't know what this means."
[ silence. the cleric does not fill it. they have been trained their whole life not to. ]
— the anchor — "...are you alright?"
[ the ritual completes. ]

The Anchor does not need to know what they agreed to. They only need to ask. What the Anchor reads in the cleric's face in that moment — the relief, the grief, the gratitude — is all the truth they are going to get. It has always been enough for people to look at this cleric and not quite understand what they are seeing, and decide to stay anyway.

ResonancePassive

Both bonded characters add the other character's Constitution modifier to their hit point maximum. The Shared Vitality Pool's maximum doubles permanently, and the Pool regenerates 1d6 HP at the start of each of your turns.

Shared FortitudeActive · Reaction

Once per turn, when either bonded character fails a Constitution saving throw, the other may use their Reaction to share the burden. Both characters use the higher of the two rolls. May be triggered after both rolls are revealed.

Stasis

Into StasisPassive · Trigger

When either bonded character reaches 0 HP, they do not fall unconscious. They enter Stasis instead. While in Stasis: Paralyzed, immune to all damage, untargetable by spells and abilities, no death saving throws. They persist indefinitely.

If both bonded characters would be reduced to 0 HP by the same triggering effect, only the one with fewer current HP enters Stasis; the other is reduced to 1 HP instead. If both have equal HP, the cleric enters Stasis. The Bond cannot suspend both at once.

What the Stased character experiences

The Stased character is present and aware — held, unable to act or reach anyone. Whatever was pressing before grows distant. Something reaches them from outside: presence. Not urgency. Weight that expects nothing in return.

If the Stased character has an active Bond, they may push impressions outward through it — not words or images, but emotional texture. Calm. Waiting. They cannot demand, warn, or instruct. Whatever they emit arrives as mood, not message.

For the cleric: the monitoring stops. The Anchor exists somewhere beyond reach — warm, continuous — and the cleric cannot lose them from here, cannot fail them, cannot perform. They would describe it later, if asked, as restful. Which is the most complicated thing they could say.

On signaling

If the cleric is the Stased character, they may choose to emit not yet — meaning: I am safe here; the cost of reaching in is real; wait. This is a choice, not a feature of Stasis itself. The cleric knows what Breaking Stasis costs. Whether they want to be retrieved, and when, is theirs to decide.

What the outside character experiences

The connection changes texture — two-way current becomes one-directional. The Stased character is present but unreachable.

The connection communicates this without words. It always has.

You could say: “Something is waiting for you. Not urgency. Not pain. Weight.” Through the connection, the outside character understands instinctively that reaching in will cost something real.

Breaking StasisActive · Action

A bonded character uses their Action and makes physical contact. The rescuer immediately takes Psychic damage equal to 3d10 + the cleric's CON modifier. The Stased character exits Stasis at 1 HP, all prior conditions removed.

On timing

Breaking Stasis mid-combat is almost never the correct decision. The Stased character is immune to all damage and untargetable — safer inside Stasis than anywhere on the battlefield. The risk: if the rescuer takes more damage than their remaining HP, they enter Stasis themselves. If the now-freed character then drops to 0, both are in Stasis simultaneously — Eternal Stasis. Before attempting rescue, confirm your HP comfortably exceeds the maximum possible cost (30 + CON modifier).

The bond's felt weight during Stasis is itself a warning. Trust it.

Eternal StasisPassive · State

If both bonded characters are in Stasis simultaneously, neither can rescue the other. They persist indefinitely — Paralyzed, immune to all damage, untargetable. They do not age, hunger, decay, or die. The Bond does not break, and any network rooted in it remains intact.

A DC 30 ritual with rare, specific components (DM-determined) may free them. Each linked member who joins the ritual lessens its difficulty, at the DM's discretion. Even so, the ritual is a kindness, not a given.

This is not a tragedy that announces itself. It arrives like a held breath — and waits.

What Eternal Stasis feels like from the inside is deliberately left undefined. Neither character can ever report it. The network registers them as present — direction, faint weight, the slow steady pulse of two people who have not let go. What the party makes of that, what they build around it, is not for the rules to say.

The level 8 benefits are large because the player surrenders all flexibility permanently — this is what the cleric always wanted, someone they cannot lose. The Asking is irreversible by design. The Breaking Stasis cost is high because the cleric can become a genuine burden: a compulsive sacrifice taken at low HP puts them in Stasis, and the group now has to pay to retrieve them from a state the cleric effectively chose. The bond no longer breaks at this level, but the compulsion remains. The cleric is still the cleric.

Flaw: Hollow Without You
Aspect-Granted  ·  Permanent
Gained at the Aspect. Cannot be removed. Offsets the power of all three Bond abilities.

The Aspect did not give the cleric their abilities cleanly. It revealed the pattern already there, and made it mechanical. The Flaw has four manifestations — each covering a different context, ensuring the cleric cannot optimise around it.

Compulsive SacrificePassive · Compulsion

When your Anchor takes damage and you are within range and able to trigger Life Transfer, you must make a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, Life Transfer triggers automatically as a Reaction. (This overrides the one-Reaction-per-round limit.) You cannot choose to fail this save.

This is not a decision. It never was.

The Ache of AbsencePassive · State

The Bond requires the Anchor to ask the cleric something personal at least once per long rest — their opinion, their feelings, whether they are alright. A logistical question does not qualify. If a long rest passes without it, the Bond breaks silently at dawn.

While no Bond is active: hit point maximum is reduced by proficiency bonus × 2, and the cleric has disadvantage on Wisdom saving throws. These penalties lift only when a new Bond is established.

The cleric cannot ask for this question. Asking dissolves the thing they need. They have to wait. They are very good at waiting.

Separation AnxietyPassive · Trigger

While bonded, if the Anchor has been more than 30 feet away for longer than 1 minute (10 rounds), make a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw or become Frightened — the Anchor is the source. Repeat at the end of each of your turns; on a success, the effect ends.

Note: Frightened prevents willingly moving closer to the fear's source — the cleric freezes rather than pursues.

The person they fear losing most is the one who locks them in place by walking away.

At Level 8, the Permanent Bond removes the distance-break condition, so the Bond never shatters — but Separation Anxiety still fires. The cleric will periodically become frightened of their bonded partner whenever they step too far away, save out of it after a turn or two, and carry on. This is not a bug.

The SecretPassive · Compulsion

The cleric cannot directly explain the Flaw — the compulsions, the bond, the need — to anyone. If they attempt to, they are immediately Silenced until the end of their next turn.

If an Anchor discovers the Flaw on their own — witnesses Compulsive Sacrifice trigger, pieces together the pattern, or the cleric confesses despite the silence — the Bond's dynamic shifts permanently. From that point, the cleric cannot establish the Bond with that Anchor unilaterally: the Anchor must consciously and verbally consent each time. Any hesitation, refusal, or "I need a moment" causes the Bond to fail. No save. No retry until the Anchor is willing.

The cleric used to be able to simply reach out and take the connection. Now they have to ask for it, without being able to explain why, and wait to see if the answer is yes.

Each clause covers a different type of scene so the flaw has no dead zones: Compulsive Sacrifice in combat, Ache of Absence in downtime, Separation Anxiety in movement and exploration, The Secret as the relationship deepens. One side effect of the Permanent Bond worth noting: once The Asking completes, the consent requirement from a Secret discovery dissolves. The bond no longer needs to be re-established, so there is nothing to refuse. The cleric stops having to ask. That, too, is what they always wanted.
